My Prompt Go?
A plain-language guide to data safety in Microsoft Foundry.

The most common question in every AI conversation is not about the model. It is about the data: who sees it, where it goes, whether it trains anything, and who is accountable. This guide answers those questions with Microsoft's documented commitments — and is honest about where the answer changes.

The model is stateless.
Everything that matters happens around it.

No prompt or completion is stored inside the model. So the real questions are about the pipeline: the filter in front of it, the monitoring beside it, the storage you choose to turn on, the geography it runs in — and which company is legally the processor. That is what this guide maps.

Follow one request

The six-step journey of a prompt.

For Models sold by Azure — which includes every Azure OpenAI model — this is what happens between the moment your application sends a prompt and the moment the answer comes back.

Your app sends a prompt

Over an encrypted connection to your Foundry resource, in your Azure tenant — authenticated with Entra ID or keys you control.

Your tenant, your boundary

Guardrails screen it

Content classifiers evaluate the prompt in real time for harmful content. Prompt Shields check for injection attacks.

Stores nothing

The model generates

Hosted in Microsoft's Azure environment. The model never talks to OpenAI's own services — not ChatGPT, not the OpenAI API.

Stateless

Guardrails screen the output

The completion is classified synchronously and filtered against the thresholds you configured.

Stores nothing

The answer returns

Back to your application. If you enabled stateful features (Responses API, threads, stored completions), that data is saved in your resource, in your geography.

You decide what persists

Abuse monitoring watches patterns

Only content that is flagged, or part of a potentially abusive pattern, may be sampled for review — AI review first, humans only as necessary.

Can be modified on request
In one sentence: your data is processed to serve your request and to detect abuse — and for nothing else. It is not pooled, not shared with the model's creator, and not used to train anything.

Data residency, made simple

Where is my data processed? It depends on one choice you make.

The deployment type you select determines where processing happens. One rule holds regardless: data stored at rest always stays in the Azure geography you designate — including uploaded files, stateful data, and the abuse-monitoring store.

Standard · Regional · Provisioned

Geography-bound

Within your Azure geography

Prompts and responses are processed inside the geography you chose. They may move between regions within it for performance and capacity management — but never outside.

Data Zone

Zone-bound

EU zone · US zone · APAC

A resource in an EU member state may process anywhere in the EU; a US resource anywhere in the US. The compliance middle ground with higher capacity than regional.

Global

Capacity-first

Any geography the model is available in

Highest quota and availability. Processing may occur in any Azure geography where the model is deployed. Data at rest still stays in your designated geography.

Claude residency note: Claude in Foundry offers Global Standard and a US Data Zone only. There is currently no EU data zone for Claude, and the "Hosted on Anthropic" option may process data outside Azure entirely. If you have EU residency requirements, choose Models sold by Azure with a Regional or EU Data Zone deployment for those workloads. Full detail: Compare hosting options for Claude models.
The question behind "does a human read my prompts?"

Abuse monitoring: what it is, who sees what, and how to turn storage off.

Abuse monitoring exists to detect recurring use that violates the Code of Conduct. It is narrow by design — and if you work with highly sensitive data, the storage and human-review components can be switched off.

1

Classify

Classifier models detect harmful text and images in prompts and completions and assign severity levels.

2

Detect patterns

Algorithms score frequency, severity and intentionality — recurring jailbreak attempts score higher than a one-off.

3

Review

Flagged samples go to AI review first; that review stores nothing. Human review only when confidence thresholds are not met.

4

Notify & act

Confirmed abuse triggers an email to you; except in severe or recurring cases, you get the chance to remediate before any access limitation.

If a human does review

Tight, auditable access

  • Only content already flagged or part of an abusive pattern
  • Authorized Microsoft employees only, via point-wise queries by request ID
  • Secure Access Workstations + Just-In-Time approval from team managers
  • For EEA deployments, reviewers are located in the EEA
  • Store is logically separated per Foundry resource, in your geography
Modified abuse monitoring

For sensitive or confidential workloads

  • Apply through the Limited Access form at aka.ms/oai/modifiedaccess; some advanced models have stricter criteria
  • When approved: no prompt/completion storage and no human review
  • Automated review may still run at request time; severe or recurring abuse can still trigger limitations
  • Content filtering can stay on — it never stores data anyway
You can verify it yourself. Once approved, confirm storage is off in the Azure portal JSON view or via CLI: the resource capability "ContentLogging": "false" appears only when abuse-monitoring storage is disabled. Verifiable evidence for your compliance records — not a statement you have to take on trust.
Safety that runs in the request path

Guardrails and Azure AI Content Safety: real-time, configurable, store nothing.

Guardrails (previously content filters) run synchronously as prompts and completions pass through. No prompt or output is stored in the classifier models, and nothing is used to train foundation models without your permission.

Harm categories

Four categories, four severities

Hate, Sexual, Violence and Self-harm are each scored on a severity scale and filtered against thresholds you configure per deployment.

Prompt Shields

Defends against injection

Scans user input and documents for jailbreak and indirect prompt-injection attacks before they reach the model — the attack surface security teams ask about first.

Protected material

Catches known content

Detects AI output that reproduces known text such as lyrics, articles and recipes — a direct answer to IP and copyright concerns.

Groundedness

Checks the answer against your sources

Detects whether a model's response is actually grounded in the material you supplied — the hallucination question, made testable.

Task adherence

Watches agents' tool use

Flags when an AI agent's tool call is misaligned, unintended or premature in the context of the user's request — built for the agentic era.

Custom categories & blocklists

Your policy, your terms

Define your own categories, upload blocklists and tune sensitivity for your industry — from financial services to K-12 education.

Two systems, not one: content filtering and abuse monitoring are separate. Filtering is synchronous and stateless. Abuse monitoring is the only component that may store a flagged sample — and it is the one you can apply to modify.
Encryption, isolation and ownership

What you control — and what Microsoft never claims.

Foundry is an Azure service, so the controls you already rely on apply: identity, network, keys and policy. Plus clear positions on the data you upload and the content the model produces.

Encrypted at rest, always

AES-256 by default on anything stored. Optional customer-managed keys for double encryption. Fine-tuned models are encrypted when not deployed.

In your tenant, in your geography

Uploaded files, vector stores, threads and stored completions live in your Foundry resource, in the same geography as the resource.

Deletable at any time

Stored data and fine-tuned models can be deleted whenever you choose. Fine-tunes are available exclusively to you.

Network & identity you govern

Entra ID, managed identity, RBAC, virtual networks and private endpoints. Azure Policy restricts which models anyone can deploy.

"On your data" never copies your data

Grounding data stays in the source and location you designate. The service retrieves relevant pieces at request time; no duplicate store is created.

Microsoft doesn't own your outputs

Microsoft's position: it does not claim ownership of your Output Content. You are responsible for third-party claims about your use — and IP rights in AI output vary by jurisdiction. Governing terms: the Microsoft Products and Services DPA.

Fine-tuning data stays yours

Training data you upload is not used to train, retrain or improve any Microsoft or non-Microsoft model, except as you direct inside the service.

Preview features: read the fine print

Models or features in preview may not support every commitment above — including customer-managed keys and abuse-monitoring practices. Confirm before production.

Your readiness checklist

Six decisions that settle your data posture.

Work through them in this order and your architecture, your controls and your contract terms fall into place.

Which model category are we using?

Sold by Azure, partner serverless, Claude, managed compute or downloaded — the processor and the terms change with the answer.

Which deployment type, and does residency require it?

Regional for strict geography, Data Zone for EU/US balance, Global for capacity. Note Claude's US-only data zone.

Do we need modified abuse monitoring?

For highly sensitive data, apply via Limited Access — then verify ContentLogging: false on the resource.

What stateful features are we turning on?

Responses API, threads, files, stored completions all persist data in your resource. Decide deliberately; apply CMK where supported.

Which guardrails and thresholds fit our risk?

Severity thresholds, Prompt Shields, protected-material and groundedness detection, custom categories — configured per deployment.

Are any components in preview?

Preview models and features may not honor every commitment. Confirm CMK support and monitoring practices before production.

Your data serves your request.
Nothing else.

Not shared with other customers. Not shared with the model's creator. Not used for training. Processed where you choose, encrypted by default, deletable on demand — with one clear exception you can name: when a model is sold and operated by a partner, the partner is the processor. Know the category, know the answer.
